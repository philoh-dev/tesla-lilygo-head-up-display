# Adjustable optical test bench

This is a printable, **bench-only positioning jig** for the measured display,
75 × 55 × 1.1 mm first-surface mirror and reused C1 combiner hinge. It is not
a final vehicle enclosure, precision optical mount, or automatically aligned
optical system. Mirror/glass focal properties and eye position are not known.

## Generate

From the repository root, with Python 3.9+:

```sh
python3 -m scripts.generate_optical_bench
```

Use `python` or `py` instead of `python3` on Windows. No extra packages are
needed for STLs. Outputs are in `build-hardware/optical-bench/`. Existing
generated files require `--force` to replace. Other files are not deleted.
`printable-parts.zip` bundles the individual printable STLs, manifest and this
guide. The non-printable assembly preview is deliberately excluded from it.

Optional thumbnail using Pillow from `scripts/requirements-assets.txt`:

```sh
python3 -m scripts.generate_optical_bench --force --preview-png
```

`ASSEMBLY-PREVIEW-DO-NOT-PRINT.stl` shows an illustrative arrangement for a
slicer/viewer. **Do not print it:** it contains multiple contacting parts in
assembly positions, not a print-ready union. No mirror, display electronics,
combiner glass, fasteners or tape are represented. The preview's positions
and 45° inclinations are mechanical examples, not ray-traced alignment.

The JSON manifest records measurements, quantities and retention limitations.
Default geometry remains within a 220 × 140 mm bed outline; allow for brims
and the printer's real usable area. Individual parts are exported flat on Z=0.
The tall combiner jaws are integral with their foot and print upright; their
saddles are open at the top. Choose supports/settings in the slicer as needed.

## Hardware and assembly

1. Attach four separate stand feet beneath the base corners for a bench test.
   The 8 mm clearance lets bolt heads/nuts sit below the base without rocking
   on the table. These blocks have no screw interface; secure them externally.
2. Put the three moving feet across the two base slots. Use **six M4 bolts,
   six nuts and large flat washers** (two fasteners per foot). Bolt passages
   and slots are 4.5 mm wide and square-ended, not threaded or countersunk.
   Select bolt lengths for the actual stack: base + foot + optional risers +
   washers + nut. Check underside projection against the 8 mm ground clearance.
3. Put matched wedge pairs below the display and mirror holders. The angle is
   the holder's back-plane inclination **relative to the horizontal base**, not
   necessarily optical incidence. Wedges are 10 mm wide and at most 40 mm
   long horizontally. Display wedges have a shorter support face than mirror
   wedges, so they do not protrude past the holder into the optical path. Set
   them about 34 mm apart across each moving foot, as in the preview.
4. Centre the display's 57 mm holder dimension across the bench. The mirror
   tray's roughly 80 mm dimension also spans across the bench. Secure the
   wedges and holders with removable external tape/straps so they cannot slide
   down the slope. The display frame still needs its own external retention.
5. Lower the combiner's bare pins into the two open saddles, then add the keeper
   strips. Secure the keepers externally around the supports. **No snap fits,
   screw clamps or angle locks are implemented.** Keeper strips prevent upward
   escape only when secured; they do not provide a calibrated friction pivot.
   The flap may need an external prop/strap to hold its chosen angle. Do not
   force a pin, compress the hinge, or use an unsecured flap.

For the mirror, contact only the back and edges with appropriate protective
pads included in the fit allowance. Do not tape, rub or clamp the first-surface
reflective coating. The tray has no front retaining lip: secure the back/edges
externally, keeping the coating and optical aperture unobstructed. The tray
allows 0.3 mm clearance per edge, not a tight glass press fit.

## Combiner geometry

User measurements are recorded as:

- Overall hinge, including both pins: **36 × 16 × 11 mm**.
- Bare pins: **Ø5 mm**. Rubber sleeves are not included in this design.
- Gap between pin roots / hinge body width: **24.5 mm**.
- Pin projection each side: **5.75 mm**, assuming symmetric pins.
- Axis centre: **4.5 mm from bottom and front** (2 mm surface gap + 2.5 mm radius).

The model assumes “gap between pins” means the body's outer faces at the pin
roots. Confirm that spacing with calipers; the fit coupon checks only the
individual pin's saddle fit, not the jaw spacing. The jaws have a **25.1 mm inside gap**: 24.5 mm + 0.3 mm clearance
on each side. Each 4 mm jaw sits entirely on its corresponding pin, away from
the hinge body. Bare pin tips remain partly exposed.

The saddle opening is **5.4 mm wide**, with its bottom at 27.5 mm above the
combiner foot underside. A nominal Ø5 mm pin resting there has its axis at
**30 mm**; the 4 mm base plate makes this 34 mm above the base top. Square
saddles, not circular bearings, permit fit testing and some lateral play.
The hinge axis is centred at X=20 mm on the foot; its body front is therefore
X=15.5 mm and bottom Z=25.5 mm in the nominal orientation. The keeper underside
is at Z=32.5 mm. Real tolerances, tape thickness and flap sweep remain unverified.
The combiner glass's size is unknown; its full collision envelope is not modelled.

## Adjustment and optical measurement

- **Position:** loosen the M4 fasteners and slide each foot along the base.
  The two slots fix transverse alignment; slots in this version do not provide
  independent lateral adjustment. Record positions from a ruler fixed to the
  base; there are no printed graduations or optical-centre markers.
- **Height:** stack chosen risers under each complete foot and use longer
  bolts. Heights are discrete and additive; recheck stability after stacking.
- **Display/mirror tilt:** exchange matched wedges. Generate another angle
  between 10° and 75° when needed, rather than assuming the default is correct:

```sh
python3 -m scripts.generate_optical_bench --angles 35 40 45 50 55 --output-dir build-hardware/bench-fine
```

For a smaller printer, `--base-length 180 --base-width 140` generates a shorter
base; reposition the feet yourself. The illustrative assembly is omitted for
bases shorter than 210 mm. `--pin-clearance 0.6` changes the total saddle-width
clearance, not the measured bare pin diameter. Other dimensions are the `Bench`
dataclass defaults in the generator.

Start with the existing working C1 glass angle. Display a border, centre cross,
corner labels and representative HUD text. Adjust one parameter at a time
from your intended eye position. Measure **display centre → mirror centre →
combiner centre** along the central light path; aim for approximately 100 mm
only after obtaining the full image and adequate head-movement range. Base
coordinates and straight-line foot spacing are not optical path lengths.

Test indoors first and then in a parked vehicle. Do not drive with this loose
bench jig. Cover the optical assembly when unattended in sunlight. These parts
are unprinted and not heat-, vibration-, retention- or optical-performance tested.

## Tests

```sh
python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts/tests -v
```

Tests verify every printable STL is a single connected closed mesh with
consistent outward winding and positive volume. Additional assertions cover
base/foot cutout volumes, bare-pin engagement, measured hinge offsets, exact
wedge angles, invalid geometry and overwrite protection. These are geometry
checks, not certification of physical fit or stability. CI discovers the tests
alongside the existing Python tooling suite.
